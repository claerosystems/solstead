## Confirmed direction

- Modular solar panel, controller containing a rechargeable battery, and one or more connected cameras.
- At launch, each controller supports up to two cameras, with a one-camera starter kit. Power, storage, and processing requirements must be evaluated for both configurations.
- The $500 one-camera starter kit does not have to include the battery and panel capacity needed for the full two-camera winter target. Adding a second camera may require a battery/panel upgrade; the starter controller must already support two cameras. The accepted two-camera autonomy and recovery targets apply to the appropriately upgraded configuration. Starter-kit autonomy and workload must be explicitly resolved rather than assumed to match the two-camera test.
- The $500 one-camera starter kit must target three days of full functionality at -20°C without useful solar input, followed by replenishment within three Huntsville winter design days while operating. Its test workload is to be defined separately. This is an unvalidated design target subject to the agreed explicit costing/tradeoff process, not a reduced winter-performance tier.
- One-camera autonomy-test workload: 100 events and 10 minutes of live viewing per day, with time-lapse and snapshot backup enabled. Retain the agreed event assumptions (10 seconds of activity, half at night, 50-second isolated clips), time-lapse schedule (one frame every five minutes for 12 daytime hours), and snapshot-first backup for all events with full-clip uploads disabled. Do not halve site activity for the one-camera configuration. Cellular conditions, snapshot size/count, illumination duration, and solar design input still require definition or measurement.
- Launch camera cable-distance target: up to 10 metres from the controller to each camera. Cable type, connector, voltage regulation, and power delivery remain to be selected and validated.
- Preferred camera connection: one Ethernet cable carrying data and standards-based PoE power. This takes priority over separate 12 V power wiring, subject to cost and power-loss validation. A camera's 12 V input remains an alternative, not the selected default. PoE generation, weatherproof connectors, and cable specification remain undecided.
- Launch solar-panel cable-distance target: up to 5 metres between panel and controller. Cable specification, connector, electrical losses, and mounting hardware remain to be selected and validated.
- Prefer the smallest practical solar panel. A separate rigid panel roughly 1 metre by 1 metre is provisionally acceptable, but a smaller panel is preferred. This is a provisional physical-size allowance, not a validated wattage, energy yield, hard dimensional limit, or acceptance of a larger panel. Winter performance and the $500 price priority still require validation.
- Installation may require an unshaded, south-facing solar panel on an adjustable mount set for winter. Use these conditions when evaluating the smallest practical panel. Exact tilt, shading assessment, mounting arrangement, and yield remain to be established; operation at shaded or poorly oriented sites is not promised.
- The controller battery must be customer-replaceable as a pack behind a gasketed service cover. The pack interface and replacement procedure must preserve weather resistance after servicing. Battery chemistry, capacity, connector, and sourcing remain undecided.
- Initial autonomy target: three days of full functionality without useful solar input, starting fully charged with two cameras connected. The test workload (events, lighting, live viewing, time-lapse, cellular signal, and temperature) remains to be defined. Battery capacity, weight, and cost are not yet validated.
- The three-day full-functionality autonomy target must also be evaluated at -20°C ambient with both cameras and the agreed baseline workload, including any battery-heating energy. Reduced-power mode or protective shutdown cannot count as meeting this full-functionality target. Cold-soak starting conditions and heating policy remain to be defined. If costing conflicts with the $500 priority, any change to autonomy or other performance requirements requires an explicit decision.
- After the three-day no-useful-solar test, target replenishing the battery to its initial full-charge level within three days while continuing normal operation, under a winter solar-input assumption to be established from Huntsville weather data. This is a design recovery target, not a guarantee during continuing overcast weather. Panel orientation, snow conditions, recovery temperature profile, and solar design input remain to be defined.
- Autonomy-test live-view workload: 10 minutes per day total across both cameras. This is a test baseline, not a customer usage limit. Longer viewing is allowed, with the app explaining the impact on battery life and cellular data. Other workload variables remain to be defined.
- Autonomy-test event workload: 100 detected events per day total across both cameras, as an initial baseline for a relatively quiet property or entrance. Separately test sustained activity and its effect on recording duration and nighttime illumination. Event duration and day/night distribution remain to be specified; the baseline is not an event-count limit.
- Autonomy-test isolated-event duration: 10 seconds of detected activity, with half of the 100 daily events occurring at night. With the accepted 10-second pre-event and 30-second post-event buffers, each isolated event produces a 50-second clip. Nighttime illumination duration and overlapping-event behavior remain to be specified; these are test assumptions, not customer limits.
- Autonomy-test time-lapse workload: one frame every five minutes during a 12-hour daytime window on each of the two cameras. This is a test baseline, not a restriction on customer-configured intervals or schedules.
- Autonomy-test backup workload: off-site snapshot backup enabled for all 100 daily events, with full-clip uploads disabled for the baseline and measured separately. Snapshot count per event, image size, cellular conditions, and destination remain to be specified; the test must provide sufficient backup allowance for the baseline uploads.
- Initial complete-system outdoor ambient operating target: -20°C to +45°C, including overnight operation and daytime solar charging. This does not authorize charging cells outside their specified cell-temperature limits; thermal management and its energy cost remain to be designed and validated. Component and enclosure choices must meet the complete-system target.
- Full winter operation is required: target year-round solar-powered normal operation at suitable unshaded sites rather than relying on routine seasonal manual battery charging. Occasional snow clearing is acceptable under the preceding recommendation. A representative winter location and solar design conditions remain to be selected. This does not establish Canada-wide winter coverage, extend the agreed -20°C ambient limit, or guarantee survival beyond the three-day no-useful-solar target. Feasibility at the $500 starter-kit price remains unresolved.
- Winter test location: Huntsville, Ontario. Loss of operation below -20°C is acceptable, but the system must recover automatically without a site visit when temperatures return to its operating range and sufficient energy is available. Cold exposure must not cause permanent damage that prevents recovery. The minimum non-operating survival temperature, recovery threshold, and cold-soak test conditions remain undecided; the operating range remains -20°C to +45°C.
- Powered-down cold-survival target: -40°C without permanent damage, followed by automatic recovery when operating temperatures and sufficient energy return. This is distinct from the -20°C minimum operating temperature. Cold-soak duration, battery state of charge, freeze/thaw cycles, and recovery thresholds remain to be defined and validated for the complete assembly.
- Below -20°C, the controller may deliberately perform a controlled shutdown: attempt a final status SMS, safely stop recording, and restart automatically after warming and accumulating sufficient energy. SMS delivery must not delay or prevent protective shutdown. Sensor placement, trigger/recovery thresholds, and the low-power wake mechanism remain to be designed; required battery protection remains active.
- Launch ingress/exposure scope: outdoor rain, snow, dust, and hose spray; submersion is not required. Validate the complete installed assembly, including connected cables, capped unused ports, and the battery cover after servicing. Exact ingress rating and test conditions remain undecided; no certified rating is claimed.
- Design for fixed installation at launch while keeping occasional relocation straightforward for one person using ordinary tools. Mounting method, component weight limits, and acceptable relocation time remain undecided; frequent portable use is not the initial priority.
- Customers must be able to install the system themselves, with app-guided camera aiming and a check of person-identification and licence-plate detail in the defined zone before setup is treated as complete. The check procedure and pass criteria remain undecided; a daylight check does not establish nighttime performance.
- Initial setup must work locally on-site without cellular service: pair the phone, aim cameras, and verify local recording. Activate remote viewing and SMS alerts once compatible cellular service is available. Local setup must not depend on a vendor account or company-operated service. The local pairing transport remains undecided.
- Target retail price for the one-camera starter kit (camera, controller, battery, solar panel): $500, provisionally interpreted as CAD from the preceding pricing discussion. Tax/shipping treatment and required margin remain undefined. This replaces the suggested C$1,000–1,500 planning range. Feasibility against the accepted performance targets has not been established; no performance requirement is relaxed by this price target.
- Launch sales channel: direct to customers. The $500 price need not support a retailer margin, but manufacturing, assembly/testing, freight, warranty, support, and business margin must still be accounted for. Production volume and cost targets remain undecided.
- Initial starter-kit cost ceiling: C$250 landed per unit, including components, assembly, testing, packaging, and inbound freight. The accepted selling-price basis is C$500 before tax and customer delivery. The remaining C$250 must cover payment fees, warranty, support, development, overhead, and profit; it is not net profit. These are targets, not validated estimates. Production volume remains undecided.
- Initial production-costing volume: 100 sellable units. Cost prototypes separately and do not assume large-volume purchasing discounts. This is a costing assumption, not authorization to manufacture or purchase units.
- Initial prototype hardware and testing budget: C$1,500, excluding development labour and certification. Prioritize validation of nighttime person/plate identification, winter energy requirements, and cellular remote access without company-operated servers before committing to production hardware. This establishes a budget, not purchase authorization.
- Target a Huntsville field trial in winter 2026–27. Commercial launch timing will be decided after validation results; no sales date is committed. Winter-performance validation is a launch condition, with any changes to accepted targets requiring explicit agreement.
- The Huntsville trial site will have someone available periodically to clear snow, inspect equipment, and retrieve it if needed. Log every intervention, its reason, and its effect on availability. Routine recording, charging, and recovery must remain unattended; manual intervention must not be counted as successful automatic recovery.
- Field-trial duration: at least eight consecutive weeks spanning January and February 2027. Supplement field observations with separate controlled tests of three-day no-useful-solar autonomy and -40°C powered-down survival; natural weather alone must not be assumed to exercise every required condition. Controlled-test arrangements and costs remain unverified against the prototype budget.
- Initial identification acceptance target: success on at least 95% of valid passes, assessed separately for person identification and plate readability in daylight and darkness (four separate categories). Define valid passes before testing from the agreed distance, zone, approach, and vehicle speed; count unreadable footage as a failure rather than excluding it afterward. Sample sizes, scoring method, person motion, and environmental conditions remain to be defined. This is a target, not a demonstrated success rate.
- Person-identification scoring must use an unfamiliar reviewer matching the recorded person to a reference photograph in a blinded test. Familiar-person recognition based on clothing or gait alone is not the selected acceptance criterion. Reference-set composition, blinding procedure, and scoring rules remain to be defined; automated facial recognition is not requested.
- Plate-readability success requires a reviewer, blinded to the expected registration, to read every registration character correctly. Partial readings and guesses do not pass the 95% target. The test protocol must collect the reading before comparing it with ground truth; automated plate transcription is not requested.
- If costing exposes conflicts, prioritize the $500 starter-kit price and negotiate launch performance changes explicitly. Customer ownership/control of data and the prohibition on company-operated services remain fixed. Previously accepted performance targets remain in force until specific tradeoffs are agreed.
- Initial launch market: Canada only. Carrier compatibility and customer-supplied plan requirements must be validated for Canada; worldwide connectivity is not a launch requirement.
- SMS alerts are acceptable at launch, with customers opening the companion app to view footage. Native app push notifications are not required at launch. SMS delivery is a candidate mechanism pending Canadian carrier, plan, and modem validation; a compatible plan may need SMS in addition to data. Remote video connectivity remains unresolved.

Measure end-to-end latency, report carrier delays separately, and retain them in the customer-experience result. Define availability conditions, sample sizes, and handling of undelivered messages before testing; this is not a guaranteed carrier delivery time.
- The cellular modem remains unlocked, but launch support may require a customer-supplied plan from a tested compatibility list. Compatibility must cover SMS delivery and remote video access without company-operated servers, not merely successful cellular attachment. No carrier or plan has yet been validated.
- Guided, one-time setup of a secure VPN connection on the customer's iPhone is acceptable for remote viewing. A direct VPN to the controller is a candidate, not a selected or validated architecture; compatible cellular reachability, endpoint discovery, and recovery remain unresolved.
- Live-view startup acceptance target: first current video frame within 10 seconds of tapping View, including VPN establishment, for at least 95% of tests with compatible cellular service available. Network conditions, sample size, and frame-freshness criterion must be defined before testing. This target does not establish ongoing streaming latency or override reduced-power restrictions.
- Remote live viewing defaults to lower resolution while local event recordings retain the quality required for identification. Provide an explicit option to request full-quality video or download an event clip. Resolution, bitrate, concurrent-stream behavior, and applicable data controls remain to be defined; the lower-resolution preview does not relax identification acceptance criteria.
- Supported launch cellular plans must be obtainable by individual customers without requiring a business or IoT account. A small initial compatibility list is acceptable. Availability of a plan meeting all connectivity, SMS, and eligibility requirements remains unverified.
- Customers own and control their data; no required vendor subscription or company-operated services.
- Remote viewing and alerts at launch. Customer-hosted infrastructure is acceptable only as a fallback for core remote access; third-party connectivity and notification integrations are deferred. Optional customer-controlled off-site storage is an agreed launch exception.
- Customers may enable off-site storage of event snapshots or clips to retain uploaded evidence if the controller is stolen. No company-operated storage is permitted. Destination, protocol, upload timing, encryption, and data limits remain undecided; no protection is promised for evidence not yet uploaded. Time-lapse remains local by default with customer-initiated export.
- When off-site backup is enabled, upload event snapshots first by default; full event clips are optional. Customers set a data allowance. Allowance scope, reset period, and behavior when exhausted remain undecided. Backup remains optional and does not change local recording or time-lapse defaults.
- When the off-site backup data allowance is exhausted, pause automatic uploads, continue local recording, and send an SMS explaining that backup has paused. Customers may explicitly authorize additional data. Allowance reset period, queued-upload handling, and treatment of other cellular traffic remain undecided; this is not yet a total cellular-bill cap.
- Off-site footage must be encrypted on the controller before upload so the storage provider cannot view its contents. Customers control the decryption keys. A customer-held recovery method is required, but its form and setup flow remain undecided; encryption does not conceal all transfer metadata.
- Before enabling encrypted backup, setup must require customers to save a recovery key outside the phone and controller, as a printable recovery code or a securely saved file. The exact format and confirmation flow remain undecided. Without the recovery key or another authorized device holding the necessary keys, footage cannot be recovered after all key-holding devices are lost.
- Recordings stored in the controller must also be encrypted to protect against theft of storage media or the controller. Recording must resume automatically after power returns; playback requires customer authorization. Key protection, physical-attack limits, and compatibility with the $500 hardware target remain to be validated. Encryption alone does not establish resistance to theft of an operating controller.
- Launch access model: one owner plus individually invited viewers, each with separately revocable access. Viewers may watch live video and recordings. Only the owner may change settings, manage keys, or authorize additional data usage. Invitation delivery, viewer count, remote enrollment, and revocation behavior remain undecided. Revocation cannot retract copies already obtained by a viewer.
- The owner controls security-alert delivery separately for each invited viewer. Viewer alerts are disabled by default; enabling viewing access does not automatically subscribe the viewer to SMS alerts.
- Local storage uses separate allocations for security event clips and time-lapse. When an allocation fills, automatically overwrite its oldest footage; time-lapse must not consume the allocation reserved for security events. Allocation sizes, configuration options, and retention targets remain undecided.
- Local event-retention target: at least seven days under the agreed workload, in addition to separate time-lapse and protected-clip allocations. Size storage at the recording quality needed for identification, including relevant overhead. Higher event activity may shorten retention; the app must show the actual history available. Storage capacity and allocation sizes remain unselected.
- Time-lapse retention target: at least 30 days at the baseline schedule of one frame every five minutes for 12 daytime hours daily, for each connected camera within the supported configuration. Show how capture-interval changes affect estimated retention; oldest frames follow the agreed automatic-overwrite policy. Frame quality, size, and allocated capacity remain to be selected and validated.
- Time-lapse export must support both a finished video and original captured frames. Prefer evaluating phone-side video assembly to limit controller processing, but processing location, file formats, export settings, and transfer/resume behavior remain undecided.
- Large remote downloads require explicit confirmation showing estimated cellular-data size before transfer. Offer local transfer when the phone is nearby. The large-download threshold, permissions for viewers, and accounting against data allowances remain undecided; estimated file size is not a guaranteed carrier-billed total.
- Download/export permission is owner-only by default and may be granted separately to individual viewers. Viewing access alone does not grant export permission. Revocation cannot retract copies already obtained and must not be represented as preventing screenshots or other capture of viewable footage. Export permission does not grant authority to raise owner-set data allowances.
- The owner may protect selected event clips from automatic deletion within a limited protected-storage allocation. When that allocation fills, the owner must export or unprotect clips before protecting additional clips. Export alone must not silently remove protection or delete the local copy; the space-release flow remains to be designed. Protected footage must not crowd out the capacity reserved for new recording.

Entry threshold, live-view restriction, recovery behavior, and measured savings remain undecided. Paused uploads temporarily remove off-site protection for new footage. This mode does not relax the three-day full-functionality autonomy target.
- Recovery from reduced-power mode must be automatic once the battery has sufficiently recharged, using a higher recovery threshold than the entry threshold to avoid repeated switching. Send one recovery SMS and resume configured recording and backup schedules, while respecting owner settings and any exhausted data allowance. Exact thresholds and treatment of queued uploads remain undecided.
- After complete battery depletion and shutdown, the system must restart unattended when solar input has accumulated sufficient energy for a stable restart. Prevent repeated boot/shutdown cycling; startup energy threshold and battery-management recovery must be validated. Normal operation and configured services must recover without an on-site visit, subject to available energy and connectivity.
- After a cellular outage, automatically resume queued off-site backups when connectivity returns, uploading event snapshots before optional clips within the remaining data allowance. Local recording continues during the outage; queued footage remains subject to local storage-overwrite rules. Existing low-battery restrictions and owner backup settings still apply. Alert backlog handling remains undecided.
- On recovery from an alert-delivery outage, send one SMS summary stating the outage period and number of recorded events instead of sending each missed event alert; then resume normal alerts for new activity. Summary recipients must follow owner-controlled alert permissions. Cellular data and SMS availability must be tracked separately so a data-only outage does not imply alerts were missed.

## Hardware candidates and validation needs

The [Reolink RLC-810A](https://reolink.com/ca/product/rlc-810a/) is a wired network camera. Reolink's [specification sheet](https://cdn.reolink.com/files/docs/specs/RLC-810A-IP-Camera-Specifications.pdf) lists 12 V DC, less than 12 W, and 48 V IEEE 802.3af PoE as an alternative. Verify the exact hardware revision's environmental specifications and whether its classified detection events are accessible locally without vendor services. Measure day and night consumption rather than treating its power rating as an average. Built-in detection requires powered operation even if recording is event-triggered.

A Raspberry Pi Zero or similar was suggested by the user for the controller. The [Zero 2 W product brief](https://datasheets.raspberrypi.com/rpizero2/raspberry-pi-zero-2-w-product-brief.pdf) lists 512 MB RAM and one USB OTG interface; it has no built-in Ethernet. Its suitability for forwarding compressed video and processing camera events requires measurement, particularly with multiple cameras, cellular connectivity, and storage. No controller board has been selected.

## Event recording implemented on the bench — 2026-09-22

The controller now records motion events using the accepted 10-second pre-event and 30-second post-event buffers, triggered by the camera's own ONVIF motion notifications. Engineering assumptions made to fill gaps the notes leave open, pending acceptance: activity resuming within the 30-second post-roll extends the same event rather than starting a new clip; cameras that never report an end of motion are assumed active for 10 seconds after each report; a single event is cut at 5 minutes and a new one started; the events allocation defaults to 20 GiB with oldest-first overwrite; the pre-event buffer lives on the SSD, not in RAM. Verified end to end on a laptop with a synthetic camera (an 8-second burst produced a 48-second clip); not yet verified against the S445, whose motion topic names, substream and codec still need checking. Time-lapse and protected clips are not implemented.

## First real event recorded on the Pi — 2026-09-22

The controller now runs on the Raspberry Pi 4 (Debian 13) as a user-level systemd service with the VIGI InSight S445 (firmware 2.5.0) wired to the Pi's Ethernet port. The Pi serves the camera its address (NetworkManager shared mode, camera at 192.168.0.127); this also currently routes the camera to the Internet through the Pi's Wi-Fi, which must be blocked before any claim of a camera without Internet access. ONVIF works with the camera's admin account: three media profiles (2688×1520 H.264 main stream at 25 fps, 848×480 substream, 640×360 JPEG), and motion arrives as `RuleEngine/CellMotionDetector/Motion` with `IsMotion`. Motion detection was off by default and had to be enabled in the VIGI app before any notifications arrived. After enabling it, a walk in front of the camera produced a 144-second 2688×1520 clip (96 seconds of reported activity) that downloaded and played in the companion app over Wi-Fi.

Resolved the same day: the camera's default main-stream encoding is H.264+ (TP-Link's "intelligent encoding"), which sends one keyframe every 12 seconds regardless of the GOP configured over ONVIF, so buffer segments were 12 seconds and the pre-roll boundary was only accurate to 12 seconds. Setting Video Encoding to plain H.264 in the camera web UI (Settings > Camera > Stream > Video, Main Stream) restored a keyframe every 50 frames and 2-second segments. Keep H.264, not H.264+ or H.265+, on any camera used with the controller. The camera was lying on its side during this test, so that footage is rotated.

## Camera isolated from the Internet; controller keeps the camera's clock — 2026-09-22

An nftables table on the Pi now drops all forwarding to or from the camera's Ethernet port and any DNS from it, persisted at boot, so the S445 can reach only the controller. Because that removes the camera's NTP source, the controller sets the camera's clock over ONVIF (manual mode) at start and every six hours, reports the observed skew, and ignores camera timestamps more than five seconds off in favour of its own receipt time. Measured skew after the change: about one second. This is the first concrete step toward the "camera with Internet blocked" test condition in the camera notes; the camera's own cloud features are expected to be unusable in this state, which is intended.

## Live view over the local network — 2026-09-22

Implemented as on-demand HLS: the controller copies the S445's 848×480 substream (512 kbit/s, keyframe every second) into one-second fragmented-MP4 segments in RAM while a phone is watching, and stops itself 20 seconds after the last request. ## CPU temperature and supply monitoring — 2026-09-22

The Pi 4's SoC caps its clock at 80 °C and throttles hard at 85 °C, so the controller treats 75 °C as a warning and 80 °C as a problem, logs crossings, and the app's health dot reflects them. It also reads the Pi's own under-voltage and throttling flags, which on a battery- and solar-fed controller matter more than temperature: any under-voltage is logged as an error and shown as "Power supply: under-voltage" until the next boot. Bench reading in a room with no case: about 50 °C. The enclosure, the -20 °C to +45 °C ambient target and the winter heater all bear on this; measure inside the real enclosure before setting the final thresholds.

## Encrypted local storage: controller side — 2026-09-22

Proposed in [ADR 0003](adr/0003-seal-recordings-to-owner-held-keys.md), pending acceptance. The controller seals each event clip to public keys enrolled from the owner's phone, in the published age file format, and holds no key that can decrypt. This is how it meets both "recording resumes automatically after power returns" and "playback requires customer authorization" without a secure element or a passphrase at boot: stealing the SSD or the whole controller yields sealed files only. The recovery key required by ADR 0002 becomes one of those keys, so off-site backup can upload the same sealed files, and any age tool opens them without Solstead. The pre-event buffer and clip assembly moved from the SSD to RAM so plaintext video never reaches the drive. Measured on the bench Pi: a 70 MB, 130-second main-stream clip sealed in 0.37 s and decrypted byte-identical. Worst-case RAM for one camera is roughly 170 MB of buffer plus 170 MB for a five-minute clip being assembled, which a 2 GB Pi 4 handles for one camera but is tight for two. Not yet done: the app does not generate or enrol keys or decrypt clips, so no keys are enrolled on the bench Pi and its 75 existing clips stay unencrypted for now; manual clips and time-lapse frames are not sealed; event times and sizes remain readable on the SSD. The recovery-key presentation and confirmation flow is still open.

## Encryption becomes the owner's choice; app side built — 2026-09-22

At the user's request, encrypting recordings is now an owner setting (a switch in the app's Settings) rather than mandatory, which replaces the earlier note that recordings stored in the controller must be encrypted. ## Restoring access on a new phone — 2026-09-22

A phone that is not enrolled (a replacement, or a second phone) restores access in Settings with the recovery key, typed from the printout, opened from the saved file, or scanned from the QR code. The app checks the key is this controller's, enrols the phone, and adds the phone to every older encrypted recording by re-wrapping only each file's small header; video is never downloaded or re-encrypted for this, and the phone does not keep the recovery key. Verified in the simulator against a bench controller with eight encrypted clips: a wrong key was refused before anything was sent, the right key typed in lower case with spaces was accepted, three clips were unlocked and five already opened on this phone, the re-keyed clips' encrypted video was byte-identical afterwards and still opened with the original phone and recovery keys, and a re-keyed clip played on the phone. QR scanning uses the camera and could not be tested in the simulator; it needs a check on a real iPhone.

## Owner can delete events; where clips live on the phone — 2026-09-22

The owner can delete an event from the app (swipe left on it, then confirm); the controller removes the clip and its record and logs the deletion in the health log. Deleting an unencrypted clip removes the file but, as with any SSD, does not guarantee the old blocks are erased; deleting an encrypted clip leaves only unreadable ciphertext behind. Event recordings live only on the controller. The phone downloads a clip to its temporary folder to play it (decrypting it there when encrypted), deletes that copy when the player closes, and now also clears the folder at launch in case the app was stopped mid-playback. ## Time-lapse and test clips encrypted; encrypted time-lapse assembled on the phone — 2026-09-23

With encryption on, time-lapse frames and manual test clips are now encrypted as well as event clips, so nothing the controller records is readable from a stolen controller or drive. The user agreed the design: each frame is encrypted as its own file when it is captured (one file per day would mean holding the day's frames unencrypted in memory until evening), at a cost of a few hundred bytes per frame. ## Telemetry: battery, solar and temperature history — 2026-09-25

The user wants the controller to log power and temperature once a minute and graph the last 30 days, as an owner feature built in stages rather than a bench-only tool. Agreed in a design session:

- **Telemetry** is its own record, separate from the health log (see CONTEXT.md). Each minute stores the average of the readings heard in it and how many there were, plus the minute's lowest battery voltage, which catches the sags that brown out a Pi. Charge state keeps the minute's last value; under-voltage and throttling record whether they happened at all.
- It is kept on the recording SSD, one file per day, never on the OS card; if the SSD is missing, minutes wait in memory and are written when it returns. The owner chooses how long it is kept in Settings: 7 days, 30 days (default), 90 days or a year. A year is about 40 MB. Shortening it deletes older days at once, after the app confirms.
- The Pi 4 has no real-time clock, so after a power cut its clock restarts at the last saved time until NTP reaches the internet (8 minutes after one bench boot on 2026-09-25). Telemetry is held in memory until the clock is synchronised and then written with corrected times, so outages never produce mis-stamped history. A DS3231 clock module is added to the hardware list; it also fixes event timestamps after an outage.
- Telemetry is stored unencrypted and served only with the owner token. This follows ADR 0003's rule for operational data: the controller must read it (reduced-power mode will act on battery history), and it reveals less than event times already do. Whether viewers see it is decided with the viewer model.
- The app shows one scrolling "Power & temperature" screen opened from the controller health section: a range picker (24 hours, 7 days, 30 days, all), then Battery (average voltage with the minute's low as a band, shaded by charge state), Power (solar in and system use in watts) and Temperature (processor, enclosure and battery, with the 75 °C and 80 °C processor limits) on a shared time axis, then daily harvested and used Wh. The controller summarises long ranges so the app stays quick over cellular. A chart appears only once its data exists.
- No alerts on telemetry in this work; threshold alerts belong to SMS alerts and reduced-power mode. The health log does record the battery reaching low voltage and the charger's load output switching off.

Hardware chosen for measurement: a Victron SmartSolar MPPT 75/15 (about 220 W of panel at 12 V, so it covers the 100–200 W panels discussed; the 75/10 stops near 145 W). The controller reads it over Bluetooth using Victron's published Instant Readout broadcast, not the VE.Direct cable; the cable remains a fallback if gaps appear. The camera and Pi converters are wired to the Victron's 15 A load output, because the charger's battery current does not include loads connected directly to the battery, so this is the only way to see system use at night without a separate shunt. The owner connects the charger in Settings: the controller scans for Victron broadcasts, the owner picks theirs and pastes its Instant Readout key, which stays on the controller. The Victron is a bench and early-customer measurement source; a production controller may measure with its own sensor behind the same telemetry. Two waterproof DS18B20 probes add battery and enclosure temperature; battery temperature matters because lithium batteries must not be charged below 0 °C.

Stages: (1) the telemetry store, retention setting, clock hold, API and app screen with processor temperature and the Pi's supply flags, needing no new hardware; (2) the two temperature probes; (3) the Victron reader, Battery and Power charts and daily energy.
