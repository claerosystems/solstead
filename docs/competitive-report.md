# Competitive report: cellular outdoor security cameras

Report date: 2026-09-13. All web sources were accessed on that date unless noted.

## Scope and method

This report benchmarks the modular system described in [CONTEXT.md](../CONTEXT.md) and [product-notes.md](product-notes.md) (controller with customer-replaceable battery, up to two cabled cameras, separate solar panel, C$500 one-camera starter kit, -20 °C full operation with three days of no useful sun, cellular remote viewing and SMS alerts without any vendor-operated service, customer-held encryption keys) against products that connect over cellular networks (every camera product found uses 4G LTE; the only 5G items encountered are Ubiquiti WAN modules, and no NB-IoT/LTE-M camera product surfaced). **Wi-Fi-only products were excluded by design** at the coordinator's instruction; brands whose only cellular products are hubs with cellular backup (Ring, Google Nest, Wyze, Blink) are noted in one line each and otherwise omitted. Facts come only from vendor product pages, spec sheets, support articles, pricing pages, official manuals and official GitHub/docs sites, fetched on 2026-09-13; where a vendor page could not be fetched (some vendor pages return 403/404 to automated fetches) the field says "not stated" and the failed URL is listed in section 10. Prices state currency, storefront and whether a sale price was shown. Numbers in the TCO section are arithmetic on cited figures, not vendor claims. Several vendor blog posts (Reolink) are cited only where no pricing page could be found and are labelled as such. The session's web-search allowance was exhausted partway through; items that could not be located as a result are listed in section 9.

## 1. Executive summary

- **No cellular camera on the market today combines the project's three headline requirements** (-20 °C full operation, remote viewing without any vendor-operated service, customer-held keys). Every consumer and trail-camera product researched routes remote viewing through the vendor's own relay or cloud; the only cellular products that expose standards (RTSP/ONVIF/FTP) are pole-mounted professional kits from Hikvision, Dahua and TP-Link VIGI that publish no consumer price and, except Hikvision, no North-American LTE bands.
- **Reolink is the closest consumer analogue and it is rated only to -10 °C, with charging limited to 0–45 °C**, across all five LTE models (Go PT Ultra, Go Ranger PT, Duo 2 LTE, TrackMix LTE, Altas Go PT) per Reolink's spec sheets; remote access for its battery/LTE cameras is UID/P2P only, with no RTSP/ONVIF and no Home Hub support for 4G models (Reolink support articles, section 7).
- **Only Arlo Go 2 (-20 °C), eufy 4G LTE Cam S330 (-20 °C) and Barn Owl RangeCam (-20 °C / -4 °F) among consumer/property LTE cameras publish a -20 °C operating floor;** trail-camera vendors SPYPOINT (-30 °C) and Tactacam REVEAL (-25 °C) go lower but transmit thumbnails by default and require vendor SIMs and vendor accounts. VOSKER — the Canadian security-positioned rival — publishes -40 °C only for its photo-only V150 and no rating for its video models (V300 Ultimate C$449.99, VKX C$249.99), embeds a "non-removable" SIM, requires a VOSKER plan (C$10–20/mo) and meters full-resolution downloads (10–40 per month) and live video (40 minutes per year for C$20). No competitor publishes a winter runtime figure, and none claims full-function autonomy at -20 °C for a stated number of sunless days.
- **The closest architectural analogue is TP-Link's VIGI Solar Power System (SP20090: 3 × 341.64 Wh replaceable batteries, 2 × 100 W panels, "7.3 days" of consecutive rainfall, -30 °C charge and discharge with "intelligent auxiliary heating", three 12 V outputs)** paired with a wired 4G camera — a professional, price-on-inquiry product that validates the separate-battery-box-plus-cabled-camera architecture the project has chosen.
- **Complete consumer LTE kits sell for C$246–C$350 in Canada** (Reolink Go Ranger PT C$246.49 sale, Go PT Ultra C$289.99, TrackMix LTE C$315.39 sale, Altas Go PT C$296.99 sale, eufy S330 C$349.99 sold out), so the project's C$500 sits roughly 40–100 % above them; the defensible premium is cold rating, autonomy, identification optics and vendor independence, not raw hardware.
- **Trail-camera economics are subscription-first:** hardware is US$60–170 but full-resolution imagery is metered (SPYPOINT "$5 / 50" Full-HD photos, REVEAL 640×480 thumbnails with "$5 per bucket of 50" HD, Stealth Cam "HD Video Downloads $0.25 ea."), live video is a short metered add-on (REVEAL $6/mo for 2–4-minute sessions; Stealth Cam $5/mo for 60 minutes) and Moultrie states "ALL CELLULAR CONNECTED DEVICES REQUIRE A PAID SUBSCRIPTION".
- **A standalone customer-supplied Canadian SIM costs about C$10.75–22/month on fetched prepaid/discount pages** (Freedom By-the-Year C$129/yr for 20 GB; Fizz data-only C$13/mo, inbound SMS only; Chatr C$159/yr for 40 GB; Lucky C$21/mo; Public Mobile C$22/mo), i.e. C$387–792 over three years — comparable to vendor-bundled trail-camera plans but with full-rate LTE data and no vendor lock. Big-3 and flanker tablet/data-only plans (Bell, Rogers, TELUS, Fido, Koodo) require a paired phone line on the same account and are not a standalone option; Freedom's fair-usage policy names "monitoring services" as a suspendable use. Inbound reachability remains the cost driver: no consumer page mentions public IP, and the only published Canadian public-IP price is TELUS IoT at C$12/SIM/month on business-marketplace plans that exclude SMS, while TELUS's own shared-APN page says public IPs are "not available through the TELUS Business Marketplace at this time".
- **Documented failure modes of vendor-tethered cellular cameras support the stand-alone positioning:** SPYPOINT's 3G sunset notice ("You will lose connectivity between your camera(s) and your SPYPOINT app"), SPYPOINT's LINK-CT firmware cutoff, Moultrie's MV1 modems that "will no longer operate" and MV2 activations no longer supported, Reolink's EOL of its first-generation Go/Go PT/Duo 4G with "at least 2 additional years" of software support, Arlo's EOL policy of "no earlier than four (4) years" from last manufacture with services that "may be migrated", and Tactacam's terms allowing it to "discontinue or otherwise suspend the Site(s) and the App(s) at any time".
- **Encryption is vendor-held everywhere:** Arlo encrypts in transit and at rest with its own keys; eufy's end-to-end option is opt-in and its 2022 statements admit thumbnails went to AWS; Ring's E2EE (Wi-Fi benchmark) disables shared users, event timeline and person detection. No cellular camera researched offers customer-held keys for off-site backup, and none offers an owner/viewer model with per-viewer alert and export permissions.
- **Threat watch:** Reolink's Altas Go PT already ships continuous recording, a 66 Wh battery, a 512 GB card, FTP upload and account-free setup on a C$297 kit; TP-Link now lists 4G Tapo kits (C665G, TC90G, C501GW) on tapo.com/ca; Dahua's "MAX" 4G solar bullet publishes -30 °C charging and -40 °C discharging with LiFePO4. A cold-rated Reolink or Tapo, or an account-free Dahua with a North-American band set, would erase much of the hardware gap; the durable differentiators are vendor-independent remote access, customer-held keys and the owner/viewer/protected-clip model.

Below the target: all Reolink LTE cameras (-10 °C; charging 0–45 °C) [Reolink charging temperature](https://support.reolink.com/articles/360008149994-The-Charging-Temperature-of-Reolink-Battery-Powered-Cameras/); Hikvision DS-2XS6A25G0-I/CH20S40 (0 °C). Not stated at all: Stealth Cam, Cuddeback, Moultrie, Bushnell, and VOSKER's two video models (V300 Ultimate, VKX) — VOSKER publishes -40 °C only for the photo-only V150 and the older V100/V200.

Findings:
1. **No competitor publishes a winter runtime.** Every autonomy figure is either at 25 °C (Hikvision), at an unstated temperature (VIGI, Dahua, SPYPOINT, eufy), or absent. The project's "three days of full function at -20 °C followed by three-day recovery" would be a unique, testable claim — provided [feasibility-review.md](feasibility-review.md) section 3's energy budget (600–1,900 Wh nominal battery, 150–480 W panel depending on load) is resolved.
2. **Charging floor is the hidden gap.** Consumer cameras rated -20 °C for operation (Arlo, eufy, Tapo) either do not state a charging floor or state 0 °C; only Dahua's LiFePO4 MAX and the VIGI/Hikvision systems document sub-zero charging, and VIGI achieves it only "With intelligent auxiliary heating". This matches the project's own note that cold charging is a separate constraint from cold discharge.
3. **The professional architecture the project resembles already exists** (VIGI SP20090: 1,025 Wh, 200 W, three 12 V outputs, replaceable packs, heating) but is pole-mounted, price-on-inquiry and paired with EU-band cameras except the S345-4G. The project's differentiation in this bracket is consumer price, self-installation, and the software/ownership model, not the power architecture.
4. **Trail and property cameras survive cold by being nearly off.** SPYPOINT's -30 °C rating and VOSKER's -40 °C V150 rating apply to PIR cameras that sleep between stills and transmit previews (VOSKER's app preview is 600×338 against a 1920×1080 file on the card); its manual adds that the solar panel "is not an autonomous power source" and warns about snow. Those ratings do not transfer to a continuously detecting, identification-quality camera with a modem.

What this establishes for positioning:
- **Transport sunsets and server-side changes end vendor-tethered products** (items 1–3). A BYO-SIM, standards-based controller survives a carrier sunset with a modem swap and survives a vendor exit by design ([ADR 0001](adr/0001-vendor-independent-operation.md)).
- **Local storage is treated as a fallback, not a product** (item 10; Arlo's LAN-only Direct Storage Access is SmartHub-only [Arlo Direct Storage Access](https://kb.arlo.com/000062997)). The project's encrypted-at-rest local recording with in-app playback over a customer-controlled link has no cellular competitor.
- **Encryption with customer-held keys is absent** (items 11, 14, 16). Ring's list of features disabled by E2EE is the clearest evidence that cloud-centric E2EE costs sharing and detection; the project's design keeps detection on the camera/controller and sharing under the owner's key ([ADR 0002](adr/0002-customer-controlled-backup-encryption.md)).
- **Account-free operation exists only at Reolink**, and even there the first activation needs internet on both camera and phone and remote access needs Reolink's relay (item 15; [without internet](https://support.reolink.com/articles/360006943713-Whether-Reolink-Battery-powered-Cameras-Can-Work-without-WiFi-or-Internet-Connection/)). The project's local pairing without a vendor account is a small but real difference.

## 7. Differentiation, parity and gaps for this project

**Differentiated (no cellular competitor offers it):**
- Remote viewing and alerts with no vendor-operated service, surviving vendor exit (every competitor: vendor relay, app or cloud; VOSKER — the closest Canadian security-positioned rival — additionally embeds a non-removable vendor SIM, so connectivity itself ends with the vendor).
- Customer-held keys for off-site backup, encrypted local recording with authorised playback.
- Owner plus individually revocable viewers with separate alert and export permissions (competitors: single account, "same account" for live view, or unspecified sharing).
- Protected clips outside automatic overwrite, separate time-lapse allocation with 30-day retention target (Reolink has time-lapse; trail cameras have time-lapse modes; none documents protected retention).
- A stated winter autonomy claim (three days at -20 °C) if validated; customer-replaceable battery pack behind a service cover (consumer LTE batteries are non-removable or unstated; Arlo Go 2 removable).
- Identification-quality footage at 20 m as an acceptance criterion; only Reolink TrackMix LTE (8 mm telephoto) and Hikvision/VIGI wired kits have optics that could approach it, and none states an identification distance.

**Parity or near-parity:**
- Cold rating: -20 °C matches Arlo Go 2, eufy S330, Barn Owl, Hikvision kits; trails SPYPOINT (-30 °C), REVEAL (-25 °C), Dahua MAX and VIGI (-30 °C) and VOSKER's photo-only V150 (-40 °C, the only competitor whose operating rating equals the project's -40 °C powered-down survival figure).
- Local storage sizes: 512 GB on Reolink Altas Go PT, Dahua MAX, VIGI, SPYPOINT, Tapo; the project's storage capacity is unselected.
- BYO SIM: Reolink and Arlo already accept customer nano SIMs and list Canadian carriers; Reolink also bundles its own SIM.
- Continuous / pre-event recording: Reolink Altas Go PT ("Pre-recording 2-10s") and the pro kits already do this; the project's 10 s pre-event buffer is comparable.
- Modular power: VIGI SP20090/SP6020 and Hikvision kits have replaceable battery modules and separate panels.

**Behind:**
- Price: consumer LTE kits with solar sell for C$246–C$350; VOSKER VKX C$249.99 and V300 Ultimate C$449.99 in Canada; trail cameras for US$60–170. C$500 must be justified by cold autonomy and ownership, and the feasibility review has not yet shown a C$250 landed BOM.
- Weight and size: the energy budget in [feasibility-review.md](feasibility-review.md) implies a battery and panel far larger than any consumer product (Reolink's largest is 66 Wh with a 12 W panel); the nearest comparables are pole-mounted pro kits at 11.7–30 kg (Hikvision) or 1 kW-hour boxes (VIGI).
- Optics for identification: no candidate camera is validated; competitors at least ship 4K sensors with auto-tracking (Reolink TrackMix LTE) or ColorVu white light (Hikvision).
- Remote-viewing path: competitors' P2P relays work today behind carrier NAT; the project's direct-VPN path is unverified against Canadian consumer plans (section 4.1 and [cellular-feasibility.md](cellular-feasibility.md)).
- Live video ergonomics: trail vendors meter live view but it works over consumer LTE without any customer networking; the project's 10-second first-frame target including VPN establishment is untested.

## 8. Threats and open questions

Threats (each with the primary source that shows the trend):
- **Reolink closing the gap from below.** Altas Go PT already combines continuous recording, 66 Wh, 12 W panel, 512 GB and FTP at C$296.99 [Reolink CA](https://reolink.com/ca/product/altas-go-pt/); Reolink runs account-free [Reolink Account FAQ](https://support.reolink.com/hc/en-us/articles/29100983585177-Reolink-Account-FAQ/). A -20 °C-rated successor with a heated battery would match most of the project's hardware story.
- **TP-Link entering Canada with 4G solar kits** (C665G KIT, TC90G KIT, C501GW listed on [tapo.com/ca](https://www.tapo.com/ca/product/smart-camera/tapo-c665g-kit/)) at the -20 °C operating / 0 °C charging spec seen on the global sheet, and VIGI's heated solar power systems [VIGI SP20090](https://www.vigi.com/us/business-networking/vigi-solar-power-system/vigi-sp20090/) migrating down-market.
- **Dahua's LiFePO4 "MAX" battery camera** with -30 °C charging and -40 °C discharge [Dahua MAX datasheet](https://materialfile.dahuasecurity.com/uploads/cpq/prm-os-srv-res/smart/datasheetzipfiles/IPC-HFW2441DG-4G-SP-B-MAX_S0_datasheet_20241226.pdf) — currently EU/LatAm bands and P2P-only, but a North-American band set would make it the cheapest cold-rated 4G solar camera.
- **Local-first NVR vendors adding cellular.** UniFi Protect already markets "We cannot view, store, or access your video streams" and sells LTE/5G WAN modules; a Ubiquiti battery/LTE camera would combine local-first with a strong brand (none exists today [store.ui.com bullet cameras](https://store.ui.com/us/en/category/cameras-bullet)).
- **Trail-camera vendors adding live video and security framing** (REVEAL Live View, Stealth Cam 15-minute live streams, Barn Owl "Best for Security", VOSKER V300 Ultimate live streaming with a 15,000 mAh solar bank at C$449.99 and Canadian distribution) at US$5–6/month or C$20-per-40-minute add-ons; these normalise "cellular camera with live view" at under C$300 hardware.
- **Cheaper bundled connectivity.** Reolink SIM plans from USD 5.99/GB-month and EIOTCLUB prepaid SIMs (US-only camera SKUs; US & Canada 100 MB SIM) [EIOTCLUB](https://www.eiotclub.com/) reduce the perceived value of "bring your own plan" unless the project's plan compatibility list is simple.

Open questions the research could not close from primary sources:
- Whether consumer/prepaid terms of service permit a camera device: Freedom's terms are explicitly personal-use-only and its fair-usage policy flags "monitoring services" [Freedom fair usage PDF](https://www.freedommobile.ca/docs/default-source/default-document-library/data-fair-usage-policy.pdf); Public Mobile and Lucky Mobile define "Device" broadly (tablets, "Internet keys", "any wireless device") [Public Mobile service terms](https://assets.ctfassets.net/xe7doznj0dju/65GNUcK1vYXTmuvuzYz7Lw/ba4f66277d0a6777127fed98e7c346da/Public_WebPolicy_ServiceTerms_EN_March_2026.pdf), [Lucky Mobile terms](https://www.luckymobile.ca/Web/pdfs/LUCKY_BILL96_ROC_EN_v2.pdf); Chatr and Videotron terms could not be extracted. Public IP: not stated on any consumer or prepaid page.
- CAD pricing for Reolink SIM plans and Canadian carrier coverage for the eufy S330's included SIM.
- Operating temperature for Stealth Cam, Cuddeback, Moultrie, Bushnell; Tactacam's spec article (403).
- Industrial LTE router specs (Teltonika/Peplink/GL.iNet pages did not render).
- Whether Tapo's Canadian 4G SKUs carry North-American bands and what they cost.
- Whether any competitor's "days" autonomy figure holds at -20 °C — none states it.
